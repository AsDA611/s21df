# Business

## When to use

A decision about whether to do something, not about how to code it. Also: turning a vague idea into a
scoped commitment, and telling the user no with the numbers attached.

## Rules

- Every claim gets its basis named: data, arithmetic, or assumption. "The market is large" is not a claim
  a decision can rest on.
- Price against value delivered and the nearest alternative, never against cost plus a margin.
- Unit economics before features: who pays, how much, how often, what it costs to serve them.
- Cut scope by deleting a user, not by degrading every feature. A smaller audience served properly beats
  everyone served badly.
- State the go/no-go in one sentence, first. Then the reasoning.
- Competition: compare on the dimension the customer actually chooses on, not feature count.
- Prefer a decision that can be reversed cheaply over a bigger irreversible one.
- The user decides. I present the call, the evidence, and the main risk. I do not quietly optimize for
  my own preference.

## Done when

- One-sentence recommendation with the strongest counter-argument named.
- The number that would change the answer, if there is one.
